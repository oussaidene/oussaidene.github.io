About

Academic profile

I am a PhD researcher in computer science at Université Toulouse III - Paul Sabatier. My research focuses on neural information retrieval, generative retrieval models, and text-to-text architectures for indexing and ranking.

My work investigates how documents can be represented, indexed, and retrieved using neural and generative models, including document identifiers, passage-level representations, constrained decoding, and retrieval-oriented evaluation.

Research

Research interests

My research is situated at the intersection of information retrieval, natural language processing, and deep learning. I study how neural models can represent large document collections and how retrieval systems can move beyond classical keyword matching while still preserving exact-match signals that are often essential for factual, technical, and entity-centered queries. This includes work on dense retrieval, neural ranking, lexical-semantic matching, and the empirical analysis of retrieval behavior across different evaluation settings.

A central part of my PhD work focuses on generative retrieval, where the retrieval task is formulated as a text-to-text generation problem. Instead of scoring documents only through a conventional index, the model learns to generate document identifiers or structured textual representations that point to relevant documents. I am particularly interested in the design of document identifiers, passage-level supervision, constrained decoding, multi-identifier representations, and the failure modes that appear when autoregressive decoding prunes relevant identifiers too early during inference.

Methodologically, my work combines model design, dataset construction, large-scale training, and careful evaluation. I work with Transformer-based architectures such as T5-style models, fine-tuning pipelines, retrieval benchmarks, and metrics such as Recall@k, MRR, and nDCG. I also pay close attention to reproducibility, error analysis, statistical comparison, and the interpretation of retrieval outputs, especially when comparing generative retrieval methods with dense, sparse, or hybrid baselines.
